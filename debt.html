Chapter 03 — The Caseworker
Meet Fatima.
She's drowning.
Help organisations exist exactly for moments like Emma's. But the professionals inside them face a different crisis — not short on expertise, but buried in paperwork a machine could handle in seconds.
✕
Emma arrives with a bag of letters — torn, photographed, mixed with old receipts.
✕
Fatima spends 47 minutes sorting: who is the creditor? what's the real deadline? is this in legal process yet?
✕
4 other clients wait outside. Two have court dates tomorrow.
✕
Fatima is brilliant at helping people. Right now the system is using her as a document scanner.
Every hour spent reading letters = one hour not spent helping people.
Chapter 04 — The System
Everyone loses.
Unnecessarily.
Emma's story is the default outcome of a system built for legal correctness, not human understanding. The cost radiates outward — touching people who never even saw the letter.
↑
Creditors spend on reminders, lawyers and collection agencies — for debts resolvable in week one with a simple payment plan.
↑
Governments absorb the downstream cost: courts, emergency social aid, healthcare strain from financial anxiety.
!
The root cause is not unwillingness. It is one letter — written for lawyers, received by humans.
The law is compliant.
The communication has failed.
These are not the same thing.
